# What is an EOA?

An **externally owned account** (EOA) is an Ethereum account controlled by a single private key. Most personal wallets are EOAs. There is no contract code behind it, no second approver, and no delay.

<Callout title="In plain English">
Whoever holds that one key can do anything the account is allowed to do, instantly. If the key is stolen, lost, or misused, nothing stops it.
</Callout>

## How we detect one

We call `eth_getCode` on the address. If there is no code, it's an EOA. Contracts we can't recognize are treated the same way until someone labels them, because we can't show they are any safer.

## Why curators care

<Callout title="Why curators care" tone="neutral">
A single key that can mint, upgrade, or seize is the most direct path to losing collateral value. It's the first thing to check before listing a token.
</Callout>

On RWABeat, a critical power or freeze held by an EOA makes the Control rating **red**. Pause or a compliance change held by an EOA makes it **yellow**. A 1-of-n multisig counts as a single key, since any one signer can act alone. Operational keys for freezing are common at regulated issuers; the question is what they can do and who watches them.
