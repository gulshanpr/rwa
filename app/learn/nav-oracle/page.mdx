# What is a NAV oracle?

A fund's **net asset value** (NAV) is what its holdings are worth per share. A **NAV oracle** is a contract that publishes that number onchain so other contracts, such as lending markets, can use it.

<Callout title="In plain English">
It's the price tag smart contracts read. If the price tag is wrong or old, every contract that reads it is wrong too.
</Callout>

## What we check

- **Who writes it:** a single key, the issuer's multisig, a single data provider, or an independent oracle network.
- **Where the number comes from:** the issuer itself, or a fund administrator or custodian.
- **How fresh it is:** the time since the last update compared with the expected cadence. Within cadence is on schedule, 1–3× is late, and more than 3× is stale.

## Stable $1 tokens

Some funds keep the token at $1 and pay yield as new tokens. Lending markets often hardcode $1 for these. That works until the fund's real value drops below $1, and then nothing onchain notices.

## Why curators care

<Callout title="Why curators care" tone="neutral">
Your market is only as safe as its oracle. A hardcoded price removes the circuit breaker; a stale feed delays it.
</Callout>
